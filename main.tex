\title{AI Capability with Allocable Capital:\\
Balanced Growth and Finite-Time Singularity}
\author{Oliver Pardo}

\begin{abstract}
This paper studies an economy in which artificial-intelligence capability is non-rival but cannot be used without capital. A single reproducible capital stock can be reallocated instantaneously among conventional production, AI inference, and autonomous AI research. This formulation prevents the same machine from entering production twice while preserving a tight feedback loop from output to accumulation, from accumulation to research compute, and from research compute to AI capability. The elasticity of substitution between effective labor and AI services, $\sigma_{XL}$, separates three regimes. When $\sigma_{XL}<1$, a regular long-run equilibrium is labor-anchored: AI services grow with effective labor and the capital shares assigned to inference and research vanish. When $\sigma_{XL}=1$, an exact balanced growth equilibrium exists under a transparent subcriticality condition and sufficient curvature restrictions. When $\sigma_{XL}>1$, capital does not automatically regularize the economy. A regular bounded-capability tail is inconsistent with the developer's costate equation, while any regular AI-dominated branch with convergent expenditure shares reaches a finite-time singularity. The last result characterizes a pre-singular solution of the decentralized necessary conditions; it is not, by itself, an infinite-horizon equilibrium or an exclusion of all irregular continuations.
\end{abstract}
